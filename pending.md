## Research Candidates (November 2026)

Ten new candidates from the industry scan in [industry-view.md](industry-view.md), one or two per proposed stage. Each was found through search excerpts only, because the publishers' sites were blocked from the research session, so **every one still fails check 3**. Still to check for each: open the page, confirm the claims below, and make the curator's call. The problem numbers refer to the ranking in industry-view.md.

- [AI Playbook for the UK Government](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-playbook-for-the-uk-government) - Government Digital Service (10 February 2025): ten principles, including meaningful human control at the right stage and bringing commercial colleagues in from the start. Stage: Intake and business case; Build vs buy. Problems 2, 5 and 7. Uptake: the UK National Audit Office's 2026 guide reportedly draws on it. Curator: decide.
- [Generative AI experiences rapid adoption, but with mixed outcomes](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/ai-experiences-rapid-adoption-but-with-mixed-outcomes-highlights-from-vote-ai-machine-learning) - S&P Global Market Intelligence / 451 Research (May 2025): the share of companies abandoning most AI initiatives reportedly rose from 17% to 42%, with 46% of proofs of concept scrapped on average; a base rate for sizing a pilot portfolio. Stage: Intake and business case. Problem 2. Still to check: the figures on the page; only the highlights are free. Curator: decide.
- [Lessons Learned from Shipping AI-Powered Healthcare Products](https://www.infoq.com/presentations/ai-healthcare-learnings/) - Clara Matos, Sword Health, QCon London (April 2025), recording and transcript: evaluations, domain-tuned guardrails and clinician review as release gates in a regulated product. Stage: Evaluation and quality. Problem 4. Uptake: an independent ZenML write-up, and QCon invited a 2026 follow-up. Curator: decide.
- [Policy for the responsible use of AI in government v2.0](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/impact-assessment-tool) - Australian Digital Transformation Agency (in effect 15 December 2025), with its AI impact assessment tool: a register of use cases, a named owner for each, and an impact assessment before deployment. Stage: Risk, governance and compliance. Problem 5. Still to check: the link goes to the tool; find the canonical policy page, and settle which deadline applies to existing use cases. Curator: decide.
- [Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI](https://www.imda.gov.sg/-/media/imda/files/about/emerging-tech-and-research/artificial-intelligence/mgf-for-agentic-ai.pdf) - Singapore IMDA (v1.0 January 2026, v1.5 May 2026): four dimensions, starting with "assess and bound the risks upfront"; the governance view, not the engineering view, of agents. Stage: Risk, governance and compliance; Delivery and agentic teams. Problem 6. Still to check: the link is the PDF; find the landing page and confirm the PDF is v1.5. Curator: decide.
- [The Enterprise AI Playbook: Lessons from 51 Successful Deployments](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/enterprise-ai-playbook) - Pereira, Graylin and Brynjolfsson, Stanford Digital Economy Lab (2026): reportedly finds most challenges organizational, not technical. Stage: Delivery and agentic teams; Cost and value measurement. Problems 1 and 3. Uptake: several independent analyses and a published critique. Caveat: it studies only successes, which is survivorship bias. Still to check: the publication month, and the figures, which differ between summaries. Curator: decide.
- [Good practice guide for organisations using AI](https://www.nao.org.uk/insights/good-practice-guide-for-organisations-using-ai/) - UK National Audit Office (15 May 2026), written for audit and risk committees: "productivity gains from AI should not be assumed"; the questions an oversight board will ask. Stage: Cost and value measurement. Problems 1 and 8. Uptake: too new for much evidence. Curator: decide.
- [How to Quantify AI ROI in Software Engineering](https://ai.engineer/talks/JvosMkuNxF8-quantify-ai-roi-in-software-engineering-stanford) - Yegor Denisov-Blanch, Stanford, AI Engineer, organizer transcript: gross output gains reportedly shrink once rework is counted, so seat counts and PR counts are not ROI. Stage: Cost and value measurement. Problems 1 and 10. Uptake: independent write-ups and a critique noting there is no preprint. Still to check: the event and date, the headline numbers (which vary between sources), and the overlap with the METR study under Left Out. Curator: decide.
- [Trust, attitudes and use of artificial intelligence: A global study 2025](https://figshare.unimelb.edu.au/articles/report/Trust_attitudes_and_use_of_artificial_intelligence_A_global_study_2025/28822919) - University of Melbourne and KPMG (2025), n=48,340 across 47 countries: reportedly, 66% rely on AI output without checking it and 56% have made mistakes at work because of AI. Stage: Adoption and change. Problems 3 and 4. Curator: decide.
- [Making AI Work: Leadership, Lab, and Crowd](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/making-ai-work-leadership-lab-and) - Ethan Mollick (22 May 2025): an operating model for AI adoption built on feedback loops between leadership, a central lab and the crowd of employees. Stage: Adoption and change. Problem 3. Uptake: adapted into a SAFe portfolio blueprint by a third party, and critiqued by Art of Lean. Caveat: a newsletter post by a well-known author; check that it has substance and not just reach. Curator: decide.
